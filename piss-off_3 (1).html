01 · The Lab

Why a nutrient lab is running a pee drive

[LAB NAME] is part of Circular Nutrient Systems, a University of Michigan effort to close the loop between what we flush and what we grow. Most agricultural fertilizer today is mined or manufactured at real environmental cost — but human urine already carries a rich, ready supply of the nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium crops need.

The Piss Off is our first stadium-scale pilot: a single home game's worth of tailgate donations, diverted before it ever reaches the sewer, treated on site, and turned into a usable fertilizer product for [APPLICATION SITE]. Every cup poured is a data point for the research.

02 · The Technology

From tailgate to topsoil

Undiluted, source-separated urine is easier to treat than the mixed wastewater it usually ends up in — which is what makes a stadium-scale collection worth doing.

01

Collect

Waterless, urine-diverting fixtures at [LOT / GATE] keep donations separate from the rest of the stadium's plumbing.

02

Stabilize

On-site treatment holds onto nitrogen and stops the odor-causing breakdown into ammonia. Method: [TREATMENT METHOD].

03

Recover

Nutrients are concentrated into a fertilizer product — [STRUVITE / LIQUID CONCENTRATE] — through precipitation and separation.

04

Apply

The finished fertilizer heads to [APPLICATION SITE] to grow the next round of crops, closing the loop.

Why urine specifically? It carries most of the nitrogen and phosphorus that would otherwise travel through energy-intensive wastewater treatment — or straight into local waterways as nutrient pollution. Diverting it at the source recovers those nutrients before they become someone else's problem.
